\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We presented a budget-aware LLM agent for provenance-graph alert investigation and \bench, a cross-platform
benchmark of 21 tasks from public Windows and Linux attack telemetry with benign look-alikes and cue-controlled
variants.
\ifnum\ResStudyAvailable=1
With Claude Opus 5.5 it reached reward \ResFullReward{} and \ResFullAccuracy{} verdict accuracy at no false
positives; the prompt's investigation method, not the safeguards, made it efficient, and per-call traces exposed
what aggregate scores hid.
\fi
\ifnum\ResCueAvailable=1
\ifconf
Renaming artefacts without changing behaviour raised missed attacks from \ResCueBaseMissed{} to
\ResMimicryMissed{} (and for GPT-6 astra from \McGptBaseMissed{} to \McGptMimicryMissed{}) without a single false
alarm -- a silent failure that shows up as cleared alerts. Neither defence we tested, canonicalising names away or
a prompt rule to distrust them, restores accuracy (\S\ref{sec:remedy}): erasing names removes the attacker's lever
but also the real-name crutch the models leaned on. The lessons are diagnostic, not a quick fix: evaluate triage
agents on renamed variants, and trust a benign verdict only when it cites behaviour, not names. Future work
includes instructions planted in telemetry, type-checked grounding, structural mimicry and SOC-analyst studies.
\else
Renaming attacker-chosen artefacts, while leaving the recorded behaviour untouched, raised the number of missed
attacks from \ResCueBaseMissed{} to \ResMimicryMissed{} for Claude Opus 5.5 and from \McGptBaseMissed{} to
\McGptMimicryMissed{} for GPT-6 astra, and lowered an open-weight model in the same direction -- in every case
without a single false alarm. That is the quiet danger of the failure: it does not announce itself as an error,
but as an alert closed and forgotten. We then asked whether the obvious defences remove it, and found that they do
not (\S\ref{sec:remedy}). Canonicalising operator names away before the agent reads the graph strips the
attacker's lever, but it also strips the real names the models had been leaning on; a prompt rule that merely
declares names untrustworthy changes almost nothing. The lessons are therefore diagnostic rather than a quick fix:
evaluate triage agents on renamed variants before trusting them, and accept a benign verdict only when it rests on
cited behaviour rather than on names. Closing the attacks that remain will require evidence the provenance graph
does not by itself carry. We leave to future work the stronger adversaries this setting invites -- instructions
planted in the telemetry and adaptive, optimised renaming -- together with type-checked grounding, open-weight and
multi-host evaluation, and studies with practising SOC analysts.
