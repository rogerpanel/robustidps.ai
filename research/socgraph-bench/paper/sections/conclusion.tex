\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We presented an LLM agent that investigates security alerts by walking provenance evidence under a tool-call
budget, and \bench, a cross-platform benchmark of 21 investigation tasks built from public Windows and Linux
attack telemetry, with benign look-alikes and selector-defined ground truth. The agent's local safeguards (a
response cache, schema validation, a grounding check and budgets) resolve avoidable mistakes before they cost a
call; parallel workers learn the runtime's limit; and rationale fields are presented in a form compatible with
model-side policies. In a pilot on the Windows tasks, every verdict was correct and wasted calls were the
largest single source of lost reward, which motivates the ablation protocol we define and release.
\ifnum\ResStudyAvailable=1
On the full benchmark, over \ResStudyRepeats{} repeats, the agent reached reward \ResFullReward{}, quality
\ResFullQuality{} and verdict accuracy \ResFullAccuracy{} with no false positives at \ResFullCalls{} calls per
task. The ablation identifies the investigation-method prompt as the component that makes the agent efficient,
and finds no measurable effect of the local safeguards with this model; its only verdict errors fell on an
ambiguous task whose twin shares its alert. Per-call traces were the decisive instrument: they showed that
the safeguards seldom fired, that most lost quality came from identifier types and from the prompt's own
efficiency advice, and that public attack recordings leak label cues the agent reads. Building the benchmark
also showed how easily ground truth can penalise reasonable evidence: two Linux defects surfaced only from
scores that did not vary across configurations.
\fi
\ifnum\ResCueAvailable=1
Cue-controlled variants showed that the agent did not need the recordings' label cues but did need the
attacker's names: renaming artefacts without changing behaviour raised missed attacks from \ResCueBaseMissed{}
to \ResMimicryMissed{}, a cheap evasion that evaluations on public attack recordings do not see.
\else
The released \emph{blind} and \emph{mimicry} variants measure shortcut reliance and lexical evasion
directly.
\fi Future work includes instructions planted in telemetry, type-checked grounding, weaker and open
models, multi-host scenarios, and studies with SOC analysts.
