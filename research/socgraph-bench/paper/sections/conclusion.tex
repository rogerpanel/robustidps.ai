\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We presented a budget-aware LLM agent for provenance-graph alert investigation and \bench, a cross-platform
benchmark of 21 tasks from public Windows and Linux attack telemetry with benign look-alikes and cue-controlled
variants.
\ifnum\ResStudyAvailable=1
With Claude Opus 5.5 it reached reward \ResFullReward{} and \ResFullAccuracy{} verdict accuracy at no false
positives; the prompt's investigation method, not the safeguards, made it efficient, and per-call traces exposed
what aggregate scores hid.
\fi
\ifnum\ResCueAvailable=1
Renaming artefacts without changing behaviour raised missed attacks from \ResCueBaseMissed{} to
\ResMimicryMissed{} (and for GPT-6 astra from \McGptBaseMissed{} to \McGptMimicryMissed{}) without a single false
alarm -- a silent failure that shows up as cleared alerts. Neither defence we tested, canonicalising names away or
a prompt rule to distrust them, restores accuracy (\S\ref{sec:remedy}): erasing names removes the attacker's lever
but also the real-name crutch the models leaned on. The lessons are diagnostic, not a quick fix: evaluate triage
agents on renamed variants, and trust a benign verdict only when it cites behaviour, not names. Future work
includes instructions planted in telemetry, type-checked grounding, open-weight models and SOC-analyst studies.
\else
The released \emph{blind} and \emph{mimicry} variants measure shortcut reliance and lexical evasion
directly. Future work includes instructions planted in telemetry, type-checked grounding, open-weight models,
multi-host scenarios and studies with SOC analysts.
\fi
